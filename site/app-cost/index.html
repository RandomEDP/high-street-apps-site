What moves the price

Why one app costs more than another.

Most of the cost of an app is the number of things it has to do on its own, rather than just show. Showing your menu is simple. Remembering who a customer is, how many stamps they have and what they ordered last time takes more building and more testing.

Things that add to the price

  • Customer accounts. As soon as people log in, the app needs somewhere safe to keep their details.
  • Taking payments. Ordering and paying in the app means more screens, more testing and Apple's rules on payments.
  • Linking to your other systems. Tills, booking systems and stock systems each work differently. Some are easy to connect, some aren't.
  • Android as well as iPhone. A second platform means a second app to build and test, or a web app alongside.
  • Content that doesn't exist yet. If your menu, photos or prices aren't online, they need to be written or gathered first.

Things that keep it down

  • Using what's already on your website. My apps are built from the content you already keep up to date.
  • Keeping the booking system you have. Linking to it costs far less than building a new one.
  • Starting small. Launch with the features you'll use every week and add the rest later.

Is it worth it?

A quick way to check.

Work out what one extra visit from a regular is worth to you, then how many extra visits a year the app needs to bring in to pay for itself.

For example, a café with a £5 average spend that gets 40 regulars to come in one extra time a month takes about £2,400 more a year. That covers a Starter app in the first year, before counting anything else the app does.

Your own numbers will be different, which is why the first step is a free concept and a fixed quote rather than a sales call.

Questions

Things people ask about price.

Is there a cheaper way to get an app?

DIY app builders cost from around £30 to £100 a month, but you build it yourself from a template and it looks like every other app made with the same tool. They suit some businesses. If all you need is a membership card or season ticket on the phone, a digital pass in Apple Wallet or Google Wallet costs from £495 to set up and £15 a month. If you want an app that feels like yours and someone to look after it, a fixed-price build is usually better value over a few years.

Why are agency quotes so much higher?

Agencies carry account managers, project managers and office costs, and often quote by the day. I design and build every app myself and quote one fixed price, which is where most of the difference comes from.

Do I own the app?

It's published on the App Store under your own business name, using your own Apple developer account.

Do I need an Android app too?

Roughly half of UK smartphones are iPhones, and iPhone owners tend to use apps the most, so iPhone is a sensible place to start. Standard and Custom include an Android version or a web app so nobody is left out.

What does the free concept include?

A short video of your app made with your own content and branding, plus a fixed quote. If you don't want to go ahead, you pay nothing.

Can I start small and add features later?

Yes. Plenty of businesses start on Starter and add loyalty, accounts or payments once they can see the app working.
